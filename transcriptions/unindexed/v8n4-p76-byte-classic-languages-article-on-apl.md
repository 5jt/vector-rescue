## Byte Magazine, December 1991, pp141 - 146

*reviewed by Adrian Smith*
{ .byline }

Whenever I see 'APL Feature' billed on the cover of a computing magazine, I get that unpleasant sinking feeling! I was half-expecting Byte to run an article on APL in its 'Classic Languages' series, and I knew that Byte was one of the few computing journals to take APL seriously, so I opened my December 1991 issue at page 141 with a mixture of anticipation and dread.

Fortunately, I need not have worried; STSC had helped them over the obvious technical hurdle by providing a reasonable typeface, and Doris Appleby did a really good job of providing the average Byte reader with a broad-brush introduction to the notation.

Her article covers the early history of APL, and reviews the current commercial applications areas. It stresses the power of APL as a prototyping tool, and mentions its recreational value for "people who get a kick out of thinking". Modern implementations (e.g. APL.68000, APL2/6000, APL\*PLUS and J) get good coverage, as does the 'A' development at Morgan Stanley. I-APL gets an honourable mention, as does APL92 at St Petersburg (the international appeal of APL is noted).

The Byte "Action Summary" concludes ...

> If you want to do complex mathematical operations in just a few keystrokes, the APL language is well suited to your needs, but it will require a modified keyboard. Nonetheless, APL is used by specialists throughout the world.

You can't say fairer than that ... well done Byte.
