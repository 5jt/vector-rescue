*by Ray Cannon*
{ .byline }

I saw a lot of reflections of Antwerp, since it rained every day I was there. Luckily I did not go for the sight-seeing, but rather for the APL, or rather, the APLers. I made a few new friends, and met many old ones, but sadly many were missing.

Most of the papers covered the “same old ground” but there were a few papers breaking new ground, in particular Pavel Luksha’s *“Probabilistic Studies of I CHING with the Help of APL”* and Gérard Langlet’s *“The APL Theory of Human Vision”*.

As for the Vendors, in the Windows arena Manugistics and Dyadic were battling it out, both giving good accounts of themselves. IBM appeared to be retreating into its OS/2 corner while Iverson Software’s J for Windows seems to be gaining ground. J Locales vs Dyadic Namespaces appears to be the next big fight, or is it between J and Manugistics over control structures?

The closing session was *“Acquisition and Processing Data from Normal and Cancer Cells”* by the Nobel prize winner Dr Ivar Giaever. As an APL consultant I found this session very interesting. More importantly however, it contained two messages to all APL vendors. If you want to sell APL (or J for that matter) to scientists doing research you must have direct IEEE support and you must have a Plot primitive. (This last point about a plot primitive was also made by Dr Walter Spunde in his paper on the *“The Impact of APL on First Year Mathematics”*.)

And finally a few key phrases.

> “The consequence of the symbols is the symbols of the consequence.”
>
> “Peace in our time, stop the war between APLs.”
>
> Lots of “Little Trojan horses” in our code.

Vendors saying that their Windows Driver was easy to learn is a bit like the Nepalese Tourist Board saying:

> “We have lots of very nice mountains, and they are all very easy to climb.”
