*From: Gérard Langlet, 12 November 1996*
{ .byline }

Among the at-least 35 pages devoted to APL96 at Lancaster in the last issue of *Vector* (Vol.13, No.2, Oct. 1996), I find no mention of Dr. Michael Zaus’ conference about *Parity Logic*, although Mike was invited jointly by BAPL and APL Club, Germany. I do not even speak of my own conferences about the *Least-Action Principle* and *Chaos Theory Revisited*. Are we included within the «etc.» p.77 of R. Procter’s paper mentioning — almost with disgust — the academic side of the APL/J community? In contradiction with what is written in the same page 77, presentations of submitted papers were not "generally poorly attended" (at least Mike’s and mine). I also remember that at APL94 in Antwerp, I had to double my workshop about Binary Logic (was APL96 as quoted p.82 — the "best conference in terms of excellent presentations and informative workshop and tutorial content..."? As an *affidatus* of APL conferences since Pisa in 1975 except Sydney and San Antonio, I really doubt it).

Was this attitude deliberate and partial, or simply the consequence of a lack of culture or a lack of interest — which corresponds to the same thing? The only word that fits what I feel is «écœuré» in French (the English equivalents that are given e.g. in the Harrap’s for the translation are indeed inadequate).

Will APL at last become a research tool in Computer Science (a genuine wish, expressed by Ken Iverson a long time ago) and even in physics, or appear as a Duck tool (I have nothing against Dyalog especially, working in fact with many implementations, including J).

Dr. Gérard A. Langlet
