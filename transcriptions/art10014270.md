*from Japan APL Association: Y. Nakano, K. Yamashita, T. Nishikawa*
{ .byline }

In J the primitive verb `p.` is for Polynomial. The monadic case of `p.` is for finding the roots of algebraic equations. This is implemented in J after Release 3 or in versions 3, 4 and 5.

For example, the solution to the cubic equation

> 5625x<sup>3</sup> + 59826x<sup>2</sup> + 339444x − 120366432 = 0

is coded in J as

```j
p. |. 5625 59826 339444 _120366432
```

The three roots are given as the second item in the boxed notation

```text
+------+-----------------------------------------+
| 5625 | 24  _17.3179j24.3248  _17.3179j_24.3248 |
+------+-----------------------------------------+
```

5625, the first item in the box, is the multiplier or the leading coefficient of the equation. That is very useful! But the use of `p.` seems to be not so popular for J users.

As far as we know, official instruction appeared only twice in the texts *J Exploring Math* (Iverson Software Inc., 1996) and *J Introduction and Dictionary* (K.E. Iverson, 1996). We suppose Dr Iverson considered that finding roots is simply an inverse of polynomials.

We, the members of the Japan APL Association and great fans of math, use the `p.` verb a great deal, because we can use it to solve algebraic equations of up to the 23rd degree.

For the cyclotomic polynomials x<sup>n</sup>−1, we have already solved them for values of *n* up to 143.

But recently, we found that the verb `p.` leads at times to fatal limit errors. For example, solving a simple cubic equation such as

> x<sup>3</sup> − 3x<sup>2</sup> + 6x − 4 = 0

by J notation

```j
p. |. y3=.1 _3 6 _4
```

causes a limit error.

To search for such examples more easily, we defined here a verb

```j
sol=:>@({:@(p. @|.))
```

instead of using the `p.` directly.

For example, `sol 1 _3 6 _4` gives a limit error.

Similar results obtain in the following examples:

- in the Cubic `(1 _3 9 _9)`, `(1 _6 6 _2)`, `(1 _6 18 _18)`, `(1 _9 9 _3)`, `(1 _9 18 _12)`, `(1 _12 6 _1)`, `(1 _12 12 _4)`, `(1 _12 18 _9)`, `(1 _15 15 _5)`, `(1 _15 30 _20)`;
- in the Quartic `(1 _10 27 _30 12)`;
- the Quintic `(1 _2 _2 4 1 _2)`, `(1 2 1 _1 _2 _1)`;
- the Sextic `(1 _3 0 6 _3 _3 2)`;
- the 8th `(1 0 1 _10 _33 _40 88 210 175)`;

and many others from polynomials derived from them.

To escape instantly from this limit error, we propose here some trials.

1. A very, very small shift in the next leading coefficient of the equation: e.g. `y3s=.1 _3.00001 6 _4`. And we solve this with `y3s` instead of `y3` by `p.`. The smaller the shift the better; even a shift as small as `1e_9` works.

2. Shift coordinate (x − r) or *f*(x−r) so as to reduce the next leading coefficient to zero; we made a verb coded in J `znc`. Then,

    ```j
    znc y3    y3z=.1 0 3 0
    ```

    We solve the new equation `y3z` using `p.`, and afterwards return `r` to recover roots for the old equation `y3`. The final roots are `1 1j1.73205 1j_1.73205`. This procedure is expressed as the verb `sol0r`. Our verbs are shown below. The names are suggestive of the roles.

3. Of course, a combination of the above two methods might be more useful. If the next leading coefficient of the equation is already zero, as above, in the 8th `(1 0 1 _10 _33 _40 88 210 175)`, we must adopt the first method i.e. the item 1.

4. We have already written several more trial verbs to replace the J primitive `p.`, and should like to contribute their details gradually in the coming journals.

J program of an example verb `sol1` among the `sol 0r` is displayed below.

```j
sol1 =: 3 : 0
r=.-0.1*(1{y)%
```

In the verb `sol1`, `r` is a shift in the coordinate variables x, and the z means *f*(x + r). Accompanying verb `horn` is omitted here, as it is a simple Horner method.
