A Note on Probabilities in the F-Distribution

Vector 10:4, page 124

mathematics and statistics

Transcribed from the printed issue; not yet reviewed. Read it in the PDF of the issue, page 124

by Keith Smillie

A recent tutorial paper by Alan Mayer in Vector on the use of APL in analysis-of-variance calculations contained a request for methods of calculating probabilities in an F-distribution which were simpler and more satisfying than the one given there. The present note gives an algorithm in J which uses the integral adverb I introduced by Kenneth Iverson in his recent monograph Calculus such that the expression f I x gives the area under the graph corresponding to the verb f from 0 to each item in the list x.

The problem is to evaluate the integral

Pr(F) = ∫0F [Γ((m+n)/2) / (Γ(m/2) Γ(n/2))] mm/2 nn/2 x(m−2)/2 (n+mx)−(m+n)/2 dx

where m and n are, respectively, the degrees of freedom for the numerator and denominator mean squares, and F is the ratio of the corresponding mean squares. In the following list the verb G is the Gamma function, B the Beta function, Fmn and Fmnx the components of the density function the first of which depends only on the degrees of freedom, and F the probability density function:

G=. !@<:
B=. (G@[ * G@]) % G@+
Fmn=. (([^-:@[) * (]^-:@])) % (-:@[) B (-:@])
Fmnx=. (] ^ <:@-:@{.@[) * ({:@[ +{.@[ *]) ^ (-@-:@(+/@[))
F=. ({.@[ Fmn {:@[) * Fmnx

For example, for 5 and 15 degrees of freedom the 10%, 5%, 2.5% and 1% points for the F-distribution are 2.27, 2.90, 3.58 and 4.56, respectively, and the expression

5 15&F I 2.27 2.90 3.58 4.56

has the value

0.8997 0.950015 0.975236 0.990321 .

The adverb I is defined by the following sequence of verbs (and one conjunction) which is equivalent to approximating the function by four groups of a polynomial of order ten:

W=. +:(#.|.@])"0 1|:@(0:,%.@(^/~@i)%>:@i=. i.@>:@+:)
ew=. +/@(-@(<:@(#@])*i.@[)|."0 1]{.~>:@([*<:@(#@])))
EW=. ([ ew W@]) f.
ai=. '+/@(x.&space * x.&[ * y.@(x.&grid))"0' : 2
grid=. space *i.@#@[
space=. ] % <:@#@[
I=. (4 EW 5) ai

The interested reader is referred to Iverson’s monograph for details.
